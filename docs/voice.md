# Voice

[Español](es/voz.md) · [Back to the README](../README.md)

Vader is meant to be heard, not read. This page covers how the voice was
chosen, how the custom voice was trained, and how speech is produced and
heard at run time.

## Choosing a voice engine

| Engine | Tried | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OpenAI TTS (`gpt-4o-mini-tts`, voice `onyx`) | May 2026 | Worked from day one; far from the character |
| ElevenLabs Flash, with streaming | June 2026 | First sound earliest; cloud only, cost per character |
| **Piper, custom voice** | September 2026 | Chosen: runs on the Pi, offline, steady timing |

A five-run benchmark on the laptop with the same 168-character text:

| | Piper (local) | ElevenLabs Flash (streaming) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| First audio available | 1.19 s | 0.53 s |
| Whole audio generated | 1.19 s | 1.67 s |
| Spread between runs | 7 ms | 458 ms to first audio |

ElevenLabs starts speaking sooner, but Piper finishes sooner, never depends on
the network and runs on a Raspberry Pi. OpenAI and ElevenLabs are still in the
code as alternatives (`VADER_TTS_PROVIDER`).

## Training the custom voice

[Piper](https://github.com/rhasspy/piper) voices are small VITS models exported
to ONNX. Training one from scratch needs many hours of audio; fine-tuning an
existing checkpoint needs far less.

1. **Sentences.** 400 sentences written for the purpose: 340 everyday
   assistant phrases (confirmations, questions, long explanations) and 60 in
   colloquial Chilean Spanish.
2. **Synthetic clips.** The clips were generated with an open-source Latin
   American Spanish TTS model, slowed to 85 % (`atempo=0.85`) for weight and
   gravity, and reviewed by ear one by one. 398 passed, for 35.9 minutes of
   audio (WAV, mono, 16-bit, 22.05 kHz).
3. **Base checkpoint.** `es_MX-ald-medium` from
   [rhasspy/piper-checkpoints](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rhasspy/piper-checkpoints):
   same `medium` architecture, same sample rate and Latin American phonemes
   (`es-419`).
4. **Fine-tuning** on a rented RTX 3090 (RunPod, about US$0.50 per hour), with
   the historic `rhasspy/piper` trainer pinned to one commit: a smoke test with
   50 clips and 10 epochs first, then 1,000 more epochs over the base
   checkpoint (epoch 9,999 to 10,999), batch 32.
5. **Export** to `es_CL-vader-medium.onnx` plus its JSON config, verified with
   SHA-256 hashes before every copy to the Pi.

The whole recipe, with preflight checks, is in
[`cloud_training/runpod_piper_v1/`](../cloud_training/runpod_piper_v1/). Two
lessons from it: the first preprocessing ran out of processes and threads
(fixed by limiting workers), and the trainer must not be swapped for the newer
GPL fork without re-validating, because the checkpoint format changed.

**Not published:** the training clips, the material used to generate them and
the resulting voice model.

**The result.** It keeps the timbre that was sought and is clearly
intelligible, but loses some naturalness and intonation. More training could
fix particular words, but Piper has a lower expressive ceiling than heavier
models, and a small dataset overfits easily. The trade-off was accepted for a
fast, local voice.

## Speaking at run time

- **One persistent process.** Piper starts once with the model loaded and
  receives one line of text per answer on `stdin`. Starting it for every answer
  meant reloading the model each time.
- **Sentence by sentence.** The answer is split into sentences (and long
  sentences at commas). A thread synthesizes them in order, and the first one
  plays as soon as it is ready. On the Pi a long answer used to take up to 4 s
  to start; its first sentence takes about 1 s.
- **Playback.** `aplay` on Linux, `winsound` on Windows. Each WAV's volume
  envelope drives the ring of the eyes while it plays.
- **Clean text only.** `response_text.py` removes markdown, lists and stage
  directions (Vader never narrates his breathing). It also enforces one
  spoken-Chilean rule: *"weón"* opens a sentence with a comma (*"Weón, ..."*)
  but closes one without it (*"... weón."*).
- **Breathing.** The first listening turn of a session starts with a
  respirator breath. Nothing is sampled from the films: the sounds are shaped
  noise generated by `scripts/generate_breathing_cues.py`.

## Hearing

- **Wake word on the device** with Vosk (`vosk-model-small-es-0.42`) and a
  closed grammar, so nothing goes to the cloud before *"Oye Vader"*.
  `scripts/wake_word_test.py` counts hits and false wake-ups.
- **End of speech** with Silero VAD: 800 ms of silence ends the turn.
- **Transcription** was chosen by benchmark with 15 of the author's own
  phrases, recorded with the real microphone and scored by word error rate,
  latency and cost (`scripts/benchmark_stt.py`). Today: `grok-stt` streaming
  first, then `gpt-4o-transcribe` with a Chilean context, then `grok-stt` over
  REST. Every request asks the gateway not to train on the audio.

Details of each step in [How it works](how-it-works.md).
